# History of Vaelora

**Status:** selected deep-world anchors and a working local chronology. No universal calendar or complete creation account is established.

## Deep history

[Colossumbra’s remains](colossumbra.md) occupy [Ru’Lora](regions/ru-lora.md). [Lumastrazil’s silence](lumastrazil.md) shapes the moon-elven direction. The source traditions also speak of lost star elves and depleted moon elves. Their relative order, causes and exact extent in Vaelora are open.

These are separate anchors until evidence or a deliberate creative decision connects them. The wiki does not collapse them into one ancient war or declare one villain responsible for every loss.

## The cultivated margin

The following sequence belongs to the proposed [Ellionar–Sereward–Bellweather trading relationship](three-measures.md), not the whole planet.

| Relative period | Event | Surviving consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Several generations before the dispute | Ellionar communities enlarge spring-fed terraces; living craftspeople replace gates on some older foundations | Gardens, workshops, repair marks and household plot rights |
| Within living family memory | The Low Spring: poor wet seasons and crop disease cause uneven losses | Abandoned plots, migrations, changed crops and disputed memories of assistance |
| After the shortage | Three settlements make the Three Measures agreement covering grain reserves, passage and water | Delivery guarantees, shared stores and maintenance obligations |
| Present local dispute | A repaired branch and changed convoy demand expose incompatible water promises | Restricted gates, stranded flour and conflicting orders at a crossing |

The [three settlements](settlements.md) remember these events differently. Some garden households connect the Low Spring to neglected shrine offerings; planting records also describe shortages and disease. Neither account is converted into a proven divine cause.

## What has not been dated

The settlement of other regions, a shared origin of elven peoples, the construction of northern observatories and any world-spanning political era remain unwritten. Ruins in paintings do not establish one vanished civilization. Add dates when they clarify relationships rather than creating a decorative list of ages.
