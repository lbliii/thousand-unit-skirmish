# Vaelora zone audio plan

[Art references](art-direction/vaelora-v1/README.md) · [Audio kit](audio-kit-plan.md) · [Audio Studio](audio-library.md)

Creative production proposal, 30 September 2026. Based on visual inspection of the ten selected zone paintings and ten ecology keys, plus their culture/lore checkpoint. No audio was generated or auditioned for this plan. Instruments, musical cultures, motifs and tempos below are proposed choices, not established canon or claims about historical musical practice.

The shared sound is **ordinary life beside immense lost power**. Keep audible wood, breath, hands, water and weather beneath occasional impossible resonance. Each region needs an inviting or useful everyday register, a discovery register and a conflict register; mystery should not turn the whole world into horror. Extend Timber & Thread rather than replace its tactile command sounds.

## Shared musical language

Use the existing D–A–E short-short-long idea as a world motif. Regional themes can change its register, spacing, answering phrase and instrumentation. Do not ask every track to repeat it constantly. Arrange the motif manually if a generated performance misses it.

Use **96 BPM, 4/4, D Dorian** for the first interoperable composition kit. Eight bars take 20 seconds. The regional pulse suggestions below are alternatives for later standalone scores; they are not ten compatible stem timelines. A region should still be identifiable when played on the common grid.

Music occupies three layers: a quiet harmonic floor, an intermittent regional lead, and optional rhythmic activity. Conflict increases motion and density before volume. Keep the midrange open for orders and alerts. Avoid wall-to-wall melody, constant cymbal crashes and trailer crescendos. Reserve a luminous high accent for discovery; avoid making it sound like a resource or objective notification.

Regions describe places. Cultures describe how people make music there. Travelers can carry musical traditions across regions, and the same landscape can contain several communities. Neither instrument choice nor ancestry assigns a player to Azure or Ember. Only Ellionar, the Meridian, the Mere and the Wayfarer traditions have specific cultural anchors in the checked-in checkpoint; other cultural directions below are proposals.

## Bellweather

**Theme: a home worth defending.** The river bends, fields, orchards, scattered settlements and broad oaks suggest a countryside that is worked and loved. Sage, cream and butter yellow become rounded attacks, warm midrange and light air above the melody.

**Style and emotion:** pastoral chamber folk; capable, companionable, lightly humorous, with room for wistfulness. The main tune should feel hummable after work, not like a royal anthem. Proposed culture: communal harvest music, workshop rhythms and small local ensembles; keep any Common Hearth association tentative.

**Instruments:** wooden transverse flute, small plucked lute, bowed viol, low wood drum, soft hand claps. Let flute carry the tune and lute answer; a little open-string buzz is welcome. Suggested regional pulse: 92–100 BPM, 4/4 with a lilting phrase rather than constant jig rhythm.

**Palette:** dry oak knocks, soft leather, barley rustle, orchard leaves, shallow river current, sparse meadow birds and distant sheep. Separate village/work spots from nature beds. An occasional old-stone resonance can suggest ruins without poisoning the warmth.

**States:** everyday uses lute and flute with breathing gaps; discovery adds one long viol harmonic beneath the familiar theme; conflict uses firm wood percussion and clipped bowed figures while preserving the melody's warmth. Recovery should feel like returning to work, not celebrating a massacre.

**Avoid:** tavern party loops, incessant birds, cartoon farm sounds, sweeping heroic brass.

## The Underbough

**Theme: paths remembered by roots.** Copper foliage, burgundy shadow, exposed roots, falls and precarious crossings suggest a place that is inhabited but never entirely easy to traverse.

**Style and emotion:** earthy woodland chamber music; intimate, resourceful, curious, watchful. Proposed culture: trail gatherings and craft music with interlocking parts passed between players. Do not establish a woodland ancestry or a Boughward monopoly through the score.

**Instruments:** low wooden flute, woody bowed fiddle, muted plucked strings, hollow log drum and seed rattle. Use short repeated questions with incomplete answers. Suggested pulse: 84–96 BPM, 4/4 with occasional 3+3+2 accents; irregularity should suggest winding paths without feeling randomly timed.

**Palette:** dry leaf skitter, branch flex, root creaks, bramble brush, damp forest air and separate waterfall beds. Keep boar and other wildlife as sparse spots. Fungi need no literal sound; use soft muted harmonics as a musical translation.

**States:** everyday is close and conversational; discovery stretches the fiddle over distant hollow taps; conflict tightens the interlocking pattern and adds low percussion. The woods become alert rather than evil.

**Avoid:** generic haunted-forest whispers, continuous animal growls, thunderous tribal drums.

## The Sereward

**Theme: water makes passage possible.** Coral rock, peach dunes and brilliant turquoise waterways make contrast the identity: dry exposure around a sheltered, active oasis.

**Style and emotion:** traveling acoustic ensemble; patient momentum, hospitality, thirst, relief and open distance. The checkpoint's Wayfarer humans and explored bull/jackal peoples share craft traditions. Propose portable ensembles suited to courtyards and caravan stops. Real instrument timbres are references for an original culture, not a claim that it reproduces one historical society.

**Instruments:** oud-like short-neck lute, breathy end-blown flute, restrained frame drum, clay pot percussion and a low bowed string. Suggested pulse: 96–108 BPM, light 4/4 with asymmetric hand-drum accents. Favor modal ornament sparingly; keep a clear phrase and generous rests.

**Palette:** fine sand over stone, cloth canopy movement, ceramic contacts, palm rustle, cistern trickle and separate oasis water. Caravan tack and pack-beast breaths are spot ingredients, not a constant procession. A very sparse clear mineral tone can translate the turquoise highlights.

**States:** everyday balances dry plucks with flowing flute; discovery lets water and resonance open the space; conflict becomes a determined traveling rhythm, keeping enough air to communicate heat and distance.

**Avoid:** stock bazaar chatter, snake-charmer clichés, indiscriminate exotic scales, perpetual howling wind.

## Ellionar

**Theme: sunlight cultivated into order.** Terraces, irrigation channels, monumental gardens, honey stone, copper and lapis call for deliberate musical architecture rather than the Sereward's portable informality.

**Style and emotion:** luminous ceremonial chamber music; civic confidence, abundance, precision and restrained grandeur. Sun-elven stepped garden cities are the established visual anchor. Babylonian inspiration belongs to the architecture checkpoint; this score is an invented musical culture, not an archaeological reconstruction.

**Instruments:** plucked lyre, low harp, paired reed pipes, copper bowl tones and soft frame drum. Stack measured answering phrases like terraces; let the ensemble sound cultivated without becoming sterile. Suggested pulse: 96 BPM, 4/4, broad Dorian phrases with a bright major-sixth color.

**Palette:** channels and garden fountains, ceramic taps, subdued copper contacts, palm/frond movement and isolated pollinator spots. Give inhabited interiors short warm reverberation; monumental spaces can have longer tails in separate assets.

**States:** everyday is an orderly plucked pattern with reed answers; discovery adds slow ascending harp and copper resonance; conflict uses dignified low ostinatos and firmer hand percussion. The identity remains stewardship and civic resolve.

**Avoid:** constant triumphant fanfares, generic desert scoring, metallic shimmer on every beat.

## The Veyrholds

**Theme: endurance across the heights.** Slate walls, cliffs, pines, fortifications and suspended crossings imply music that can carry across distance but stays close to material labor.

**Style and emotion:** rugged mountain ensemble; steadfast, exposed, practical, occasionally lonely. Proposed culture: communal indoor string playing and sparse outdoor signal traditions. An alpine landscape does not establish a particular real-world nationality.

**Instruments:** low fiddle, rough plucked zither, wooden flute, deep skin drum and restrained natural horn. Use open fifths and firm short phrases, with the horn rare enough to preserve gameplay alert recognition. Suggested pulse: 88–96 BPM, grounded 4/4.

**Palette:** pine wind, gravel movement, dry stone contacts, bridge timber strain, distant falls and individual goat/raptor spots. Separate high exposed air from sheltered valley and interior beds.

**States:** everyday favors zither and fiddle around a steady low pulse; discovery exposes a long open interval and distant horn color; conflict adds weight and stubborn repetition without a cinematic brass army.

**Avoid:** nonstop war horns, drinking-song caricature, loud gusts swallowing commands.

## The Pale Meridian

**Theme: small warmth beneath a measurable sky.** Snowfields, violet distance and immense observatories need a contrast between the exposed plateau and inhabited rooms. The stars suggest patient pattern rather than constant sparkle.

**Style and emotion:** sparse astronomical chamber music; clarity, wonder, solitude and shared warmth. Star-elven instruments and warm communal interiors are anchored in the checkpoint, but living enclaves remain an RTS adaptation proposal. The score must not silently settle their survival. Include summer daylight as a possible variant rather than eternal night.

**Instruments:** plucked zither, low bowed viol, wooden flute and sparse tuned glass or silver bell tones. Let measured high notes sit over a warm acoustic bass; use long rests. Suggested pulse: 72–84 BPM standalone, or half-time phrasing on the shared 96 BPM grid.

**Palette:** restrained plateau air, snow grain, distant ice strain, wool movement and separate hearth fire. Observatory mechanism ticks are optional designed spots, not proof of an ancient network or active machinery in every ruin.

**States:** everyday is intimate zither and low strings; discovery widens into glass intervals and a slow measured figure; conflict adds a low repeating pulse and taut bows while keeping high frequencies sparse. Hearth recovery restores human proximity.

**Avoid:** endless Christmas bells, science-fiction computer beeps, supernatural choirs, permanent blizzard.

## The Siltmouths

**Theme: life by the returning tide.** Braided estuaries, pale mud, reeds, exposed roots and small crossings suggest flexible patterns rather than fixed marching lines.

**Style and emotion:** tidal acoustic music; adaptable, unhurried, communal, slightly weathered. Proposed culture: boat repair, landing-place gatherings and practical river navigation. Do not infer a named people from the landscape. Work-song phrasing can inform instrumental call and answer without generating lyrics.

**Instruments:** woody reed pipe, small plucked strings, muted fiddle, hollow wood blocks and brushed hand drum. Suggested pulse: 80–92 BPM in gentle 6/8 for standalone music; use triplet-inflected phrases in the first common-grid kit. Musical breath follows the tide without trying to sync to literal water cycles.

**Palette:** reed movement, gentle tidal wash, mud suction, hull/jetty creaks, rope strain and isolated waterbird calls. Keep wet transients soft and distinguish them from resource confirmations.

**States:** everyday has a rocking plucked figure; discovery suspends the rhythm into reeds and low bows; conflict becomes a nimble interlocking wood pattern. Use purposeful movement rather than sea-battle bombast.

**Avoid:** pirate music, generic nautical accordion, constant gull cries, huge surf on sheltered water.

## Vesperra

**Theme: a living canopy with its own attention.** Pale trunks, deep petrol green, violet fungi and immense falls suggest richly layered life, not a dead haunted wood. The paintings lean magical; keep that feeling selective.

**Style and emotion:** organic chamber textures with elusive melody; fecund, absorbing, curious, watchful, occasionally disorienting. Proposed culture: careful listening and small acoustic exchanges beneath the canopy. Supernatural inhabitants in the ecology key do not establish a settled civilization or spoken language.

**Instruments:** low breath flute, muted plucked zither, bowed strings, soft wooden slit percussion and a faint processed overtone layer. Create the strange color by transforming accepted acoustic sources in editing. Suggested pulse: 76–88 BPM, or spacious common-grid phrases; overlapping patterns can drift melodically, but exports need a stable timeline.

**Palette:** canopy air, leaf drips, distant falls, small branch movement and sparse insect/wing spots. Generate invented creature calls separately and label them designed fantasy sounds. Do not turn every luminous fungus into a bell.

**States:** everyday is breathing and alive; discovery lets a familiar acoustic note acquire an unfamiliar tail; conflict narrows the harmony and concentrates low wood patterns. Preserve beauty under threat.

**Avoid:** tropical stock-jungle cacophony, monster roars as ambience, constant shimmer, indiscriminate whispering.

## The Sombral Mere

**Theme: reflection after an unanswered song.** Pearl and lavender water, nocturnal flowers, islands and lunar gardens suggest stillness with delicate movement inside it.

**Style and emotion:** reflective nocturnal chamber music; tenderness, poise, grief, patient hope and incompletion. Moon-elven traditions responding to Lumastrazil's silence are anchored in the checkpoint. Surviving communities are a proposal; use a remembered musical tradition without implying a thriving moon court. Grief should coexist with life rather than dictate every moment.

**Instruments:** low harp, solo bowed viol, breath flute and a single rounded glass tone. Let an answering phrase arrive late or remain unresolved. Suggested pulse: 72–84 BPM, slow 4/4; long suspended tones above a restrained D/A floor.

**Palette:** gentle lake laps, shore leaves, separate nocturnal insects/waterbird spots and a soft designed opal resonance. Reflections translate into delayed musical answers, not literal echo on every sound.

**States:** everyday supports patient harp with a small warm melody; discovery opens a suspended interval without full resolution; conflict adds restrained low bows and a heartbeat-like wood pulse. Recovery returns the missing answer only partially.

**Avoid:** continuous lament, ghost voices, generic fairy chimes, horror drones indistinguishable from Ru’Lora.

## Ru’Lora

**Theme: living life at the edge of something that should not be entered.** The painting's emerald fringe and charcoal petrified interior must become two related palettes. Ivory remains, salt, dust and the immense dead form provide physical scale; silence provides emotional scale.

**Style and emotion:** fringe music is cautious but alive; interior music is sparse material resonance. Awe, trespass, toxicity, loss and restrained dread. This is Colossumbra's petrified night jungle, not an inhabited demon town. Do not invent local festive music, demon markets or routine sacrilegious visitors. A traveler may bring an external melody that fragments as they approach.

**Instruments:** fringe uses wood flute and low plucked strings; interior uses bowed stone-like textures, low contrabass harmonics, dry ceramic friction and rare cold glass resonance. These are sound-design materials, not a new ancient instrument canon. Suggested pulse: fringe 84–96 BPM; interior largely unmetered. Make any composer-grid version a fixed-length textural section.

**Palette:** fringe leaves, trickling water and sparse living fauna; interior thin air, fine dust across stone, salt grit and distant mineral strain. Huge cavity resonances should decay into absence. Avoid continuous sub-bass that exhausts speakers and the listener. Living fringe ecology remains a proposal.

**States:** everyday at the fringe retains a fragile melody; discovery into the interior removes more elements than it adds; conflict uses dry fractured pulses with long spaces, and one controlled low swell for exceptional scale. Release returns air and a small living sound, not a demonic victory choir.

**Avoid:** constant screams, chanting demons, trailer impacts, literal heartbeat everywhere, making the whole region uniformly dead.

## Palette inventory for construction

The zone sections define the creative target. This inventory defines ingredients we can request and assemble. Numbers are accepted editorial targets; one generation may yield no acceptable asset.

For each zone collect:

- **Two environment beds:** one terrain/weather bed and one water, shelter or contrasting subregion bed; 30–60 seconds each as a production target. Keep wildlife, music and activity out of them. For Ru’Lora explicitly use living fringe and interior; add separate water later if needed.
- **Three spot families:** vegetation/weather detail, fauna where appropriate, and material/craft detail. Aim for three usable variants per family. Ru’Lora's interior fauna slot becomes mineral/dust detail; do not invent living interior animals.
- **Three instrumental phrases:** lead question, lead answer and low support; four or eight bars on the agreed grid. Preserve isolated source performances where obtainable.
- **One rhythmic ingredient:** a restrained four- or eight-bar pattern with no melody, plus individual dry hits if useful.
- **One rare signature:** an isolated regional resonance or articulation; no embedded ambience and no gameplay notification rhythm.
- **Three reference arrangements:** everyday, discovery and conflict, initially 40–60 seconds. These establish the intended mix and can supply editable sections; independently generated arrangements are alternative performances, not synchronized stems.

Start with Bellweather, Ellionar and Ru’Lora: they test ordinary warmth, cultivated splendor and dangerous absence. Next use the Sereward/Meridian/Mere to check that three kinds of spaciousness remain distinguishable; then Underbough/Veyrholds/Siltmouths/Vesperra. This is an audition order, not permission to generate the complete inventory.

Keep the ensemble consistent throughout.

For discovery replace the everyday instruction with “Reduce percussion, widen the space, and introduce [RARE SIGNATURE] sparingly.” For conflict use “Increase low rhythmic motion and articulation; retain this region's ensemble and restrained loudness; no constant crashes or battle sound effects.”

### Isolated instrument template

> Isolated solo [INSTRUMENT/TIMBRE], dry close recording, no accompaniment, voice, percussion or ambience. Play one restrained [QUESTION/ANSWER/SUPPORT] phrase for [ZONE], targeting [FOUR/EIGHT] bars at 96 BPM in 4/4, D Dorian. [ZONE ARTICULATION]. Clear start, complete natural decay, space between phrases, no dramatic swell.

Use this as a music-performance brief where supported. If generation adds accompaniment or fails the grid, keep it as a reference or reject it; do not label it an isolated stem. Short single-note articulations may be requested as sound effects, but that does not guarantee tuned or tempo-accurate material.

### Environment bed template

> Continuous [SINGLE ENVIRONMENT PROCESS] in [SPECIFIC LOCATION], [CLOSE/DISTANT] perspective, quiet and evenly textured. Only [DESIRED MATERIAL]. No music, voices, footsteps, animals, machinery, dramatic events or sudden foreground transients. Suitable for a seamless repeating ambience bed, stable level and perspective.

Examples: Bellweather's soft wind through barley; Sereward's sheltered cistern trickle; Meridian's restrained plateau wind; Mere's gentle lake lap against a still shore; Ru’Lora interior's thin air brushing dry mineral grit. Use the actual looping control if available, then still inspect the seam.

### Material or wildlife spot template

> One isolated [ACTION/SOUND] of [MATERIAL OR CREATURE], [PERSPECTIVE], [TEXTURE AND WEIGHT]. Immediate clear onset, full natural tail, otherwise silence. No voice, music, environmental bed or additional actions.

Request one action per take, not a montage of nine variants. For invented fauna describe the audible mechanism and register: e.g. a small wing flutter or a low two-part birdlike call. Do not rely on a generated specimen name to specify a recognizable sound. Keep such outputs marked as fantasy designs.

## Assembly and audition

Import original recordings with exact prompts, generation IDs, dates and rights/provenance metadata. Keep dry originals and edited versions separate. Name zone assets `tus_<zone>_<family>_<action>_<variant>_v001`; use `ru-lora-fringe` and `ru-lora-interior` for that zone's two subpalettes.

Measure the actual BPM, pitch and phrase length before importing musical clips into a shared composition. Audio Studio's BPM defines placement; it does not stretch or retune recordings. Align/edit material externally when necessary, or use an accepted full mix. Only layers derived or arranged on one verified timeline should play as simultaneous musical stems. Keep natural tails outside any tight loop or crossfade them deliberately.

Build a 60–90 second audition per pilot zone: landscape bed → everyday music → discovery → conflict → release. Also audition the bare environment and the music alone. Compare all three zones at equal perceived loudness with the same command/alert sequence laid over them. A dense battle should remain understandable with music enabled.

Accept a palette when the region has an identifiable material and musical character at low volume; its three states retain that identity; ten loop repetitions have no click or obvious seam; mono/laptop playback remains clear; and commands/results stay recognizable. Describe each rejection concretely: unwanted voice, wrong material, excessive reverb, overbusy melody, inaccurate grid or too much similarity to another zone.

The current runtime supports optional packs and authored map compositions. This plan does not implement automatic zone changes, adaptive combat scoring, environmental creature emitters or additional gameplay rules. Any future local ambience or tension selection must use visible/public information and preserve the existing cue budgets and caption policy.
