## Visual direction

Build an original, inviting frontier fantasy from moss meadow, worn earth, cinder, weathered stone, timber, and slate. Detailed fantasy illustration is the target: painterly environmental cutouts and live models with authored material color, broad painted value shapes, and selected visible detail under warm, restrained light. Faceted geometry can support the silhouette, but units should not read as undecorated placeholders. Keep seams, wear, grain, and surface variation at a scale that survives the ordinary and strategic game views; judge detail at zoom `0.91` and `0.48` before adding finer texture. Avoid grimdark, neon, photorealism, and recognizable franchise shapes. Azure and Ember are match identities, not separate lore or cultures.

The medium and the finish are separate choices. Fixed-view cutouts suit static environment objects and resource states. Live geometry suits units and buildings whose facing, pose, anchors, or named parts change during a match. Blender can author live models or source renders for sprite atlases; neither medium defines the style. Any medium change needs a same-camera comparison, complete direction and action-state coverage, and renderer-side memory and frame-budget evidence. Age of Empires: Definitive Edition documents rendering 3D-modeled objects to 2D sprites for a 2D isometric engine ([official art pipeline](https://www.ageofempires.com/news/age-empires-definitive-edition-3d-2d-game/)); Blizzard documents reviewing stylized Warcraft III models for scale, color, readability, and silhouette ([official art process](https://news.blizzard.com/en-gb/article/23150111/tales-from-the-smithy-reforging-the-night-elves)).

For water and shoreline work, use the current muted blue-green water references and windworn sand rather than introducing a bright cyan accent. Main currently colors water footprints `#286173` with `#3E6570` / `#4D7982` variation; the Forked Vale preview legend uses `#304F62` with a `#62869A` stroke, and the sand ground is `#AC936D`. The environment guide calls for low-colored water footprints and soft connected ground-brush edges; a finished shoreline palette has not been separately authored. The renderer candidate at `aa07ed3` adds a soft edge band using a muted cool shore `#82968E` and the existing sand tone; that code has not had a runtime visual review. Keep obstacle and walkability meaning in the existing gameplay data.

## Camera, scale, and identity

- Use the fixed oblique orthographic camera. The current renderer starts at zoom `0.91`, supports strategic zoom `0.48`, and permits close inspection at `2.3`. Judge gameplay readability at `0.91` and `0.48`; close views cannot rescue a shape that fails there. Runtime appearance frames use a 1280 × 720 viewport.
- One world unit is one map cell. The authored sample uses Y-up, +Z forward, and stable ground-center origins. A unit body is about 0.8 world units tall. Barracks and Archery Range occupy 3 × 3 cells.
- In-world team hues are Azure `#5AA7D7` and Ember `#E67A5E`. Reserve brighter variants for HUD/minimap overlays. Keep shared materials neutral; reserve amber for resource/objective meaning and lime for selection or interaction.
- Use color and shape together. Azure uses a straight-cut pennant with one centered bar; Ember uses a forked-tail pennant with a split mark. At strategic zoom, use a square Azure marker and diamond Ember marker, plus full `AZURE` / `EMBER` HUD labels. Do not put text on individual units.
- Roles remain recognizable by equipment silhouette: Worker—compact body, pack, broad tool; Infantry—upright spear and six-sided shield; Archer—narrow profile, bow, quiver. Town Center—wide hall with taller rear tower; Barracks—enclosed gabled roof and gate; Archery Range—open canopy and front target.
- A v1 preview manifest estimates the Worker body at roughly 12 pixels high at zoom `0.91` and 6 pixels at `0.48`. Keep world scale. If the full meshes lose role readability, use the renderer-owned batched role LOD and separate team markers; do not add per-unit scene objects or raise the zoom floor.

## Ownership

- Environment owner: ground and cutout sources, resource/construction variants, provenance, and individual review frames.
- Unit-character owner: Worker, Infantry, and Archer source, GLB parts, equipment silhouettes, team cues, action poses, and source review frames.
- Building-architecture owner: structure geometry, ownership cues, construction/damage states, Blender source, GLB parts, anchors, manifests, and source review frames.
- Technical Art and Surfacing owner: shared painted materials, UV/export conventions, atlas validation, and repeatable sample previews in coordination with the character/building and renderer owners.
- Renderer owner: runtime loading/batching, authoritative state mapping, fog filtering, animation/LOD, capture harnesses, and performance evidence.
- Interface owner: cursor integration, icon placement, visible labels/accessibility, MIME and static-asset allowlist, and focused browser evidence.
- Art direction: cross-pack palette, material finish, role/team readability, and visual acceptance after pixel review.
- Each owner keeps scoped changes separate, records evidence and limits, and coordinates any shared schema or material change with affected owners.
