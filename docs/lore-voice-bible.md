# Vaelora voice bible

Draft derived from the author's writing, 30 September 2026. The [source study](lore-research.md) identifies manuscripts, draft differences and gaps. This guide describes tendencies to develop, rather than prescribing every story's tone. It is a writing reference; the [game bible](game-bible.md) remains the authority for product intent.

The author's direction is deep creative influence with freedom to transform the source material. Carry forward the sensibility and inventiveness; select literal names, events and cosmology deliberately. *The Soul Bandit* is a confirmed reference, currently available through secondary project notes rather than its original prose.

## The voice we should carry forward

The strongest shared quality is a materially strange world observed by people with appetites, vanities, grudges and limited information. Wonder arrives through contact: grit, cold, weight, taste, a ruined body's awkward handling, the smell of food. Institutions have polished language and self-serving incentives. Characters rarely have the whole explanation. Their practical responses make the world feel inhabited.

The range matters. Dreadvael is an investigation full of social resistance and uncertain expertise. Colossumbra is desperate, bodily and mythic. Container 67 can be casually funny and unexpectedly kind. Vaelora should make room for all three, with inviting places and competent everyday work alongside dangerous remains.

## Phrase and rhythm

**Give landscape behavior.** The original Dreadvael's graveyard grows like tree rings; a manor's ground and hydrangeas seem to want to swallow it. Colossumbra's opal light gropes through darkness. Objects do narrative work before anybody explains them. Use this selectively so that a scene has a clear physical image.

**Move between rich description and a small flat response.** A swollen paragraph can end on something as simple as an inconvenience or a material fact. Dialogue often lets one person explain confidently while another remains concerned with what they must actually do. Preserve the rhythm without making every ending a punchline.

**Let viewpoint distort the inventory.** Jean's beautiful university is also funding, accreditation and insularity; Vivian's ominous underground is also unsuitable footwear. What people notice reveals their priorities. Avoid a neutral narrator delivering the same encyclopedia entry through every character.

**Favor exact materials over decorative grandeur.** Copper, brass, onyx, jade, lavender wax, salt, spores, fabric and living gems appear as components, obstacles or clues. A useful detail can suggest a whole craft. Repeated declarations of ancient, forbidden, eternal and legendary cannot substitute for that work.

**Keep the physical indignity of power.** Vel must chew resistant god-flesh. Jean needs a tarp and an unfortunate family trunk. These are unusually good tonal anchors: immense stakes coexist with bodily difficulty and household consequences.

For RTS writing, compress the same technique. Give the player the immediate objective in clear language, then one revealing detail. A three-paragraph loading screen should not be required to learn which crossing to hold.

## How the humor works

The original's humor often comes from disagreement about importance. Garrett turns abnormal livestock into a theory about coffee; Charlotte remembers family obligations during Jean's investigation; a researcher sees a marvelous discovery where Jean sees immediate peril. No universal comedian narrates over them.

Container 67 widens this range. A stranded fugitive needs food; the clerk understands the bad day and quietly helps. An agent notices a suspect's attractiveness while his partner keeps the investigation moving. The humor can reveal tenderness, distraction and competence as well as cynicism.

Use underreaction, misplaced expertise, literal bargaining, bureaucratic certainty and incompatible priorities. Let characters answer differently. Profanity belongs to stress and temperament; it need not carry every joke. Give dread scenes enough uninterrupted space for danger to matter.

Avoid training on the Dreadvael rewrite's repeated references to familiar media, constant wisecracking or standard magical-destiny language. Those are observable differences from the original, not an accusation about their author. The original's subtler social mechanics are the default reference.

## Naming patterns

| Register | Observed examples | Working rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Human names | Jean Imke, Charlotte Dussault, Akar Shalhope, Kotta Mekles | Mix familiar personal cadence with an idiosyncratic surname. Names can imply different regional histories without explaining those histories immediately. |
| Inhabited places | Mumbles, Trivitt, Wallow's Ennd, Aerkinhaven | Allow short, ordinary, weathered names. Not every town needs to sound sacred. A name gains force from its use and associations. |
| Mythic or ancestral names | Colossumbra, Lumastrazil, Soman’ar, Ellion’ar | Sound and semantic echoes can suggest scale, light or lineage. Reserve apostrophes for an identifiable naming family; do not add them as a general fantasy marker. |
| Plants and materials | nightwhisper, jadesip, boilblum, susscrown, niptullegrum/bonesheer | Combine tactile vernacular names with specialist alternatives. A dual name can expose who knows, trades or studies the thing. Its use gives it credibility. |
| Coined conditions or concepts | dreadvael, dangrefoole/dangrefoolishness, zovustro, the Unwritten | An unfamiliar word earns its place through consequence and context. Explain it when it changes what somebody can do. |
| Institutional satire | Biolympus, GUARANTOR.8, post-beyond patty | Confident commercial language can expose absurdity. For Vaelora, adapt the mechanism through guilds, titles and official claims, rather than importing spaceport branding. |

The existing Bellweather, Underbough, Sereward, Siltmouths and Veyrholds names already offer a useful plainer register. Retain them while we learn what people actually call their towns, occupations and neighbors. Do not rename the whole atlas simply to demonstrate activity.

Keep a small name record: pronunciation, spelling, referent, regional language family if known, specialist/common form and source. Decide whether Ellionar names a place while Ellion’ar names a people. That distinction is a proposal until confirmed. Check near-duplicates aloud before introducing another important name.

## World-building habits supported by the prose

**Knowledge is unevenly distributed.** Dreadvael's university, miners and ritual practitioners know different things. Container 67's civilization operates ancestral machinery without full comprehension. Institutions protect knowledge and sometimes confuse status with understanding. Expertise should still be real and useful in its domain.

**Magic has relationships and procedures.** Bargains, substances, invitations, interrupted rites and jurisdiction matter. A demon can refuse on religious grounds despite a contract. This gives powers limits beyond an arbitrary energy meter. Do not conclude that all magic comes from one standardized contract system; the original distinguishes traditions and alchemy.

**The past persists in inconvenient forms.** Ruins, illness, customs, gems and equipment remain even when explanations fade. Their effects can be encountered without solving the universe.

**Progress has both accomplishments and costs.** The writing is suspicious of easy promises, but the result should not be that every innovation fails or every institution is secretly malicious. Kotta's achievements and the food clerk's kindness keep the world from becoming one-note.

**Ecology participates.** Sunstones contain life; grass and insects have relationships; a difficult landscape resists the character. Useful flora needs habitat, handling and consequence, not just a magic adjective.

## Writing checks

Before accepting a passage, answer these questions:

1. What does somebody need here, and what physical detail makes this place particular?
2. Does the detail reveal use, history or viewpoint, rather than merely ornament?
3. Who knows this claim? Is it an author's fact, a character's belief or a rumor?
4. Does a joke emerge from these people and this situation? Have they all begun to sound alike?
5. Does a new name earn its space, remain pronounceable and have one clear referent?
6. Does power have a stated boundary, obligation or consequence in this scene?
7. Can a player understand the immediate action without reading background fiction?

## Calibration samples

The [L1 package](lore-foundation-m1.md#three-calibration-passages) supplies longer ordinary, dangerous and mythic passages for user calibration. Those samples are new fiction, not quotations from the author's manuscripts.

The following are newly written exercises, not manuscript quotations, approved names or canon. They test the recommended voice in short game-facing forms.

**Map introduction:** The upper sluice has been closed since harvest. Both villages have arrived with soldiers and copies of the same water agreement. Secure the crossing and hold it until the relief convoy arrives.

**Object description:** A bowl carved from petrified wood. It remembers the shape of a tree, though its owner mostly remembers dropping it on his foot.

**Short exchange:** “Does it keep the dead out?” “No. It tells us when they come in.” “Then why have you put it in the kitchen?” “That is where we are.”

These need the user's calibration before they become a production standard. The voice guide should be revised when Crawl, Bloomers or preferred manuscript versions become available.
