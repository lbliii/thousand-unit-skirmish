# Vaelora — first lore foundation

Review draft, 30 September 2026. **L1: a world we can begin writing in.** This is the first writing milestone described in the lore conversation, separate from the roadmap's gameplay M1. It supplies a creative foundation, historical spine, three settlements, two institutions and three calibration passages. New names and events below are proposals, internally consistent within this draft; they become selected canon through explicit decisions. No new gameplay, published map placement or species history is implied.

Read with the [voice bible](lore-voice-bible.md), [research and sources](lore-research.md), [strategy](lore-strategy.md) and [selected art](art-direction/vaelora-v1/README.md).

Later direction on 30 September 2026 shifted the deliverable to a connected reference. The [lore wiki](lore/README.md) is now the current entry point; this package preserves the earlier writing milestone. Its proposed scene/scenario next steps are optional, not the current lore plan.

## The creative foundation

Vaelora is a world in which people have made good lives among things larger and older than their explanations. A pear orchard can be worth protecting without containing a secret. A god's remains can change the soil without explaining every war. The pleasure of being here matters: shade after travel, excellent bread, a clever repair, a festival somebody has been preparing for all year.

Power meets the body. Salt draws moisture from a tongue; luminous stone must be kept alive; a borrowed voice comes back hoarse. Magic enters through particular substances, acts, places and relationships. Traditions have different methods and boundaries. Knowledge travels through apprenticeship, experiment, theft, hospitality and badly remembered instructions. Skilled people can accomplish remarkable things, and can be wrong outside their expertise.

The world has both deep time and living time. Old catastrophe remains in a landscape; last season's successful repair changes who can plant this season. People inherit consequences rather than a complete account. They can preserve, adapt or abandon an inheritance. Their choices remain theirs.

Humor follows attention. An official notices a missing seal; a grower notices dying trees; a visitor notices that the official has taken the only chair. Warmth can be quiet and practical. Fear gets room to develop without a joke cancelling it.

Cultures are learned and lived. Ancestry can matter to a tradition without prescribing an individual's profession, allegiance or character. A region contains several ways of living; institutions contain dissent. Familiar everyday names share the world with stranger names reserved for things that have earned them.

The player's first encounter should be legible: a place, a need, a choice. Deeper writing reveals why that need exists and what people disagree about. Lore supports the reusable RTS core through scenario identity and presentation; this foundation requires no additional simulation systems.

### Three boundaries for supernatural writing

- State an observable effect and a useful limit before relying on a power to resolve a conflict.
- Distinguish successful practice from its explanation. A rite may work while its practitioners disagree about why.
- Keep consequence specific. No single ancient network, secret god or mastermind owns all histories.

## A historical spine

These are relative layers, with no invented universal calendar. The corridor events are new fiction. Ru’Lora and the lost elven traditions remain sourced anchors whose complete relationship to this history is open.

The Three Measures agreement covers grain reserves, passage guarantees and water delivery. Its water provisions are now incompatible: an older clause allows the caravan branch to open for a fixed period; a later addition guarantees a minimum delivered quantity. A leak had wasted water between the shared spring reservoir and the branch outlet. Repairing it improves delivery, but crews now keep the branch open beyond its old period to fill larger convoys under the quantity guarantee. Both branches draw from the same limited reservoir; the extra withdrawal leaves less for the garden rotation. The repair exposed a longstanding overpromise during a poor season. It did not mysteriously reduce upstream flow by conserving water.

Neither document is forged. Separate surveys measure different things: release at the reservoir and arrival in the caravan cistern. A joint test could establish losses and available supply. It cannot decide whose promise should be broken before the next water turn.

### Public explanations and open mysteries

Garden households describe the Low Spring through family stories, planting records and arguments about diverted water. Some believe abandoned shrine offerings weakened the springs. A shrine keeper may sincerely accept that belief while also organizing effective repairs. The draft establishes neither supernatural punishment nor a secretly malicious priesthood.

Travelers call Ru’Lora a place the dead have refused to finish with. Scholars dispute that phrase. The selected physical hazards remain; the account of Colossumbra's fate, Lumastrazil's silence and the status of lost elven peoples is not settled here. This corridor can be written without deciding who imprisoned a deity or asserting surviving star-elven enclaves.

## Three places along the route

The route is a proposed commercial relationship, not a surveyed line on the atlas. Ellionar and Sereward's local water relationship requires later placement at a plausible dryland margin. Bellweather supplies grain by overland travel, not by the same river. Do not place these names on Forked Vale automatically.

### Pellit — a Bellweather market town

Pellit grew around a weighing yard beneath three broad oaks. Rain-fed barley farms, pear orchards and sheep pastures surround it. Households keep kitchen gardens; the town maintains wells and a millpond. A good autumn smells of pear skins drying on racks and wet wool brought too close to a stove.

Pellit exports grain, felt, seasoned handles and sharp pear vinegar. It needs replacement metalwork, some salt, and reliable passage for bulky cargo. Grain brokers buy copper gate fittings from Ellionar and caravan crews bring desert ceramics. No food-growing region is entirely self-sufficient.

The yard assembly elects a weighkeeper for a season. Its practical authority comes from agreed weights, public tallies and the ability to settle delivery disputes. Tenant growers can speak, but storehouse owners can afford to wait through a poor price. That imbalance troubles Nella Imbray, a miller whose family rents its land. She wants small growers to pool their deliveries rather than bargain separately.

During the annual First Loaf supper, every oven contributes bread made from the new crop. Bakers argue over which loaf deserves the central board; children rearrange the labels. Weddings frequently combine households from the road and the farms. Bereaved families receive cooked meals and help with the next essential field task; grief should not cost them a harvest.

Pellit's immediate interest is to reopen the route. The flour already loaded for Tassel's Ennd is drawing damp under damaged wagon covers; the larger grain reserve remains sound. Broker Deren Voskel advanced payment from the Fellowship reserve for this delivery. He expects the returning convoy to bring metalwork before the mill's worn shaft collar fails. If the wagons turn back, the town faces both a missing reserve and a mill repair it cannot complete on schedule. Deren has concealed the size of the advance from Nella, who agreed to contribute flour on a smaller understanding. His insistence that a promise survives a bad market is sincere and convenient. Neither the whole town nor all its growers belong to one faction.

### Istrava — an Ellionar garden settlement

Istrava occupies a sequence of inhabited terraces, not an enormous empty monument. Honey-colored walls hold beds, courtyards, workshops and cisterns. Lapis tiles mark public channels where water can be checked without entering a household. Copper fittings are valuable enough to repair repeatedly. Shade cloth and fruit trees soften the steps.

Its households grow orchard fruit, vegetables and a locally adapted grain whose planting dates follow the spring's behavior. They export glazed vessels, preserved fruit and precisely made fittings. Pellit's grain gives gardeners room to cultivate more valuable crops; it does not mean they have forgotten how to feed themselves. Caravan trade brings salt, fibers and news.

The terrace council assigns maintenance turns and arbitrates household access. An elected gatewright can stop an unsafe gate despite a councillor's wishes. Apprentices learn by measuring flow, clearing silt and repairing wood before they handle the more exacting garden practices. Public rituals thank the sun and mark planting periods; observance varies among households.

One small horticultural tradition makes **hushlime**. A prepared limewash, set with copper and applied at dusk, cools an enclosed seed chamber beyond what ordinary whitewash achieves. It must dry fully before seeds enter; wet material blisters skin and spoils seed. Its effect lasts several nights, can be measured and does not summon rain or multiply food. This is a new magical proposal, not a manuscript recipe or implemented crop mechanic. Gardeners disagree whether its action comes from the retained dusk, copper or the preparation's living ferment.

At the Lantern Pruning, neighbors eat on the terrace steps while experienced growers trim fruit trees by suspended lights. Visitors may help gather cuttings, but are discouraged from improving the branches. A child's first successful graft is occasion for a small household feast. Elderly residents teach and keep access to shaded communal rooms rather than being treated simply as keepers of lost secrets.

Gatewright Sava Mekren wants the competing measurements tested together, but she countersigned the repaired outlet as ready for use before reviewing the later guarantee. A survey may damage the reputation on which her office depends. Councillor Orel Tavin has ordered the next garden turn honored in full: newly grafted trees on the lowest productive terrace may not survive another missed watering. Downstream households resent both officials for discussing principles while their allotments shrink. Sava is an Ellion’ar practitioner; household ancestry elsewhere remains open until developed deliberately.

### Tassel's Ennd — a Sereward caravan stop

The name comes from the old practice of tying a cart's worn tassels to a beam when its crew retired from long travel. The beam has been replaced twice. The tassels have not all survived, whatever the keeper tells visitors.

Tassel's Ennd has a small resident population and a changing company of travelers. Its drinking supply comes from a protected fresh spring, roof catchment and stored deliveries. A channel from Istrava supplements the caravan cistern used for pack animals and other agreed purposes. It is separately managed and does not receive water from the cold saline pool outside town. That pool supplies specialist salts; it is unsuitable for drinking.

The settlement repairs containers, harnesses and carts; sells ceramic storage jars; and provides lodging and guides. Irrigated plots support fresh food in modest quantities. Grain deliveries feed residents and guests. Tubers with violet flesh appear here as a provisional adaptation from the recovered Soul Bandit notes: their exact crop, growing conditions and original name await the story's prose. No new field-guide species is canonized by the menu.

Resident households choose a cistern keeper, while crews appoint a seasonal road speaker. Decisions about visitors' water require both. This protects residents from being outvoted by a passing convoy and prevents one household from charging arbitrary rates at a vital stop. Arguments remain about sick animals, debt and travelers who cannot pay.

Rumi Dassel, the cistern keeper, reserves a drinking allowance for anyone arriving on foot. Caravan proprietor Tessa Orven supports the custom but opposes extending free pack-water to commercial rivals. Retired guides worry that more traffic will consume shade, grazing and patience faster than the settlement can restore them.

Hospitality begins with a cup and a place to sit. News is exchanged over toasted grain and salted fruit, with a guest permitted to decline questions before eating. A departing crew carries a repaired jar or mended strap to a household farther along the road: a small favor that keeps distant acquaintances alive. Children learn routes as sequences of places where somebody will recognize them, before they learn distances.

## Two institutions in tension

These are corridor institutions, not new playable factions. They provide a concrete test of themes tentatively associated with the Common Hearth and Open Passage. They neither settle those factions' names nor divide ancestry into opposing teams.

### The Store Fellowship

Growers, millers, garden households and storekeepers maintain shared emergency stocks and purchase replacement tools together. Membership requires a stated contribution in goods or labor, with concessions for hardship. Settlement delegates choose purchasing officers; members can inspect store tallies. The Fellowship's promise is that a bad season need not destroy a household.

Its strength is patient investment: stores, training and maintenance carried across seasons. Its weakness is the tendency to treat long residence as proof of responsibility. Migrant workers can contribute for years while having less say than established households. Nella campaigns for membership tied to contribution and dependence rather than property.

In the dispute, Fellowship delegates want garden supply restored before guaranteeing convoy quantities. A minority argues that cutting off the road will consume the emergency stock they are trying to protect. Its guards protect stores and work crews; their presence does not establish a new unit type or special economic rule.

### The Joined Road

Guides, carriers, lodging houses and trading partners recognize reciprocal obligations along the route. A crew earns standing through completed deliveries and witnessed assistance, not through ancestry. Road speakers settle claims at scheduled meetings; serious sanctions include refusing a carrier's guarantees, which can strand their business.

Its promise is that a person can arrive somewhere new and still have recognized obligations and help. Its strength is coordination across distance. Its weakness is that prominent carriers can define acceptable risk for people who do the carrying. Tessa wants reliable minimum water quantities; hired drivers want limits on the loads proprietors send into a shortage.

The Joined Road demands the caravan cistern's guaranteed amount and offers to fund a joint survey. Some crews oppose armed escorts; others say their contracts are worthless without them. Pellit merchants participate in both institutions. Neither coalition can solve the crisis by identifying the other as foreign.

### The immediate conflict

Orel has ordered the caravan branch closed until Istrava's next garden rotation finishes. Rumi refuses to admit another large convoy without enough pack-water to send it onward; watering it from the protected drinking reserve would put residents and travelers on foot at risk. Pellit's flour wagons have already reached the crossing. Both institutions sent guards to protect crews and cargo, initially without orders to fight.

Tessa has now instructed her escorted repair crew to reopen the branch at the hour stated in the older agreement, while claiming enough duration to meet the later quantity guarantee. She knows this will violate Orel's closure order. Her alternative is to hold an undersupplied convoy through another hot day, with sick animals and drivers she has already asked to accept a heavier load. Orel orders his guards to prevent entry to the gatehouse. The first violence follows incompatible commands at a physical chokepoint. It is preventable, and specific people choose the risks that lead to it.

The immediate clock is the next garden turn and the convoy's remaining pack-water. Deren's concealment can break trust among allies, but exposing it does not create more water. A survey can improve a settlement without resolving the immediate choice. Neither institution's entire membership becomes an enemy because its officers have escalated.

A first scenario could place the player with a Pellit escort holding the road crossing while the dispute threatens the delivery. The road crossing and gatehouse must be distinct locations: controlling the road protects access but does not settle water rights. The player has a limited protective mandate, not permission to conquer either settlement. Before implementation, verify that an existing objective can represent this mandate and explain defeat without relying on mass slaughter of civilians. Negotiation, survey systems and branching outcomes remain writing possibilities. A provisional introduction is: **The caravan sluice is shut, and both councils have sent guards. Keep the crossing open for the flour wagons.** This is a story brief, not a claim that convoy or civilian rules exist in the current game.

## Three calibration passages

New fiction written for this milestone. These test distinct registers and invite concrete corrections to cadence, humor, naming and atmosphere.

### Ordinary conversation — the weighing yard

Nella had swept the pear skins from the scale twice. A third batch had arrived on Deren's boots.

“How much of this is flour?” she asked.

“All of it.”

She lifted the mouth of a sack. Beneath the white fold, something brown and jointed withdrew into a seam.

Deren leaned over it. “That much is a beetle.”

“I weighed these yesterday. They were dry.”

“The covers tore.”

“You told me you had new ones.”

“I said they were paid for.”

Nella lowered the sack. Across the yard, the drivers were sharing a loaf with the boy who had come to collect their gate fee. He held the money in one hand and the bread in the other, unable to find a decent place for either.

“Take the good sacks,” she said. “Leave these with me.”

“We haven't agreed a price.”

“Then bring the sacks back. I need the sacks.”

He waited, looking for the remainder of the bargain.

She reached for the broom.

### Dangerous encounter — beneath the garden wall

The channel had been empty when Sava entered. She had hung the red work-cloth across its gate wheel, as she had taught her apprentice to do, and crawled beneath the first brace with one shoulder against stone still warm from the day. The lamp stood behind her in the dry sediment.

Now a narrow black line divided the dust ahead of her.

She touched it with the end of her measuring rod. The wood came back wet. Somewhere beyond the next bend, a gate was lifting.

“Close the upper feed,” she called.

Her apprentice's reply arrived from the shaft above, stretched thin by the distance. He was going for the key.

The brace pressed against her back as she tried to turn. Water reached the lamp and made a small, violent sound. She moved it onto the ledge. Its light caught a white patch under the broken masonry: old hushlime, sheltered from weather, still drawing beads of moisture from the air.

The wash should have been exhausted years ago.

She kept her hand away from it and pulled herself toward the shaft. Her foot found the measuring rod. It rolled beneath her heel, and for a moment there was nothing in the passage that would bear her weight.

Above her, the apprentice dropped the key. It struck the stone twice.

“Leave it,” she called. “The side board. Push it across.”

Wood scraped above her. He was still at the shaft. Whoever had lifted the gate had either missed the work-cloth or chosen to ignore it; she could ask which when she was out.

*Author note: water is the immediate hazard. The failed work signal needs a specific cause if this scene enters the story; do not imply sabotage and forget it. The old wash's preparation date must be established before its persistence is confirmed as an anomaly. Rescue remains in progress rather than being credited to a second person entering the same hazard.*

### Local myth — the woman who lent the spring her name

At Tassel's Ennd they tell of a woman who found a spring walking away beneath the sand. She followed the damp ground until it spoke from under her feet.

It said it had forgotten where it was supposed to rise.

She gave it her name, so travelers could ask for it, and stayed until the first water came through. Then she went home. Her sister opened the door and knew her face, but could not remember what to call her.

The woman returned to the spring. By then there were six animals drinking and a man washing a bowl. She asked for her name back.

The spring offered her half. She refused: her mother had chosen the whole of it.

At last they agreed that the spring would keep the name through the dry season and return it when rain came. In the meantime her sister would call her in for supper with a bell.

Some tellers say she married the man with the bowl. Rumi says this is the part added by people who have never had to wash one. Her own grandmother ended with the first rain and the woman waking to hear her name spoken in the next room.

At the first wet-season gathering, households ring a small bell before they eat. Many do it because their families always have. Children are told to leave a cup outside for somebody still on the road.

*Author note: this is a proposed local story, not an established explanation for the actual spring. It adapts the source writing's relationships and costs of power, and comparative myth's conditional return. It is newly written, not an ancient translation. Exact spring behavior and a historical woman's existence remain unset.*

## Milestone evidence and next decisions

The review package is delivered: all five promised components exist, the settlements exchange specific goods and obligations, each institution has useful work and internal dissent, and the three passages test warmth, immediate danger and myth. This proves a usable writing foundation. It does not prove user calibration, selected canon, runtime appearance or a playable authored scenario.

The next user review should identify the strongest and weakest passage, any names that feel wrong, and whether this mix of everyday warmth and material strangeness feels like Vaelora. Then revise the package and select a small set of world facts in the game bible. Exact map placement, hydrology, faction identity and the anomalous wash need decisions before their dependent implementation. The first next production outcome is a scenario brief with an existing objective and a bounded visual inventory.
