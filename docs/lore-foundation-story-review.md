# L1 adversarial story-lead review

30 September 2026. Review of the [first foundation](lore-foundation-m1.md), followed by a focused revision. Editorial judgments below are hypotheses about story effectiveness, not new source research or claims of user approval.

## Verdict

Useful foundation; insufficient dramatic identity in its first version. The settlements were pleasant and plausible, but much of the document could describe any carefully designed fantasy trading corridor. Its strongest distinctive elements were embodied magical practice, the spring lending story, and hospitality expressed through small acts. The next draft must earn a reader's interest through specific people making costly choices. Increasing the number of gods would not solve this weakness.

## What survives the challenge

The spring-name myth is the best compact expression of the proposed world: a strange transaction changes an ordinary household, several tellings coexist, and a material custom survives. Preserve the sister and the bell. Avoid treating a later explanation as automatically better than the story's uncertainty.

The caravan drinking reserve creates a meaningful limit on kindness. The towns' seasonal pleasures give the conflict something worth losing. Competent repair and shared stores should remain real achievements, even when individual decisions fail.

## Next story test

Write one scene spanning the attempted reopening: Tessa gives the order, Sava faces the crew, and a driver or garden household bears the cost. Choose one viewpoint rather than distributing fair explanations among everyone. End with an irreversible change in trust or position. A reader should be able to say who wanted what, why waiting failed, and which choice made things worse.

Then test a second scene outside the dispute, with wonder or pleasure as its primary purpose. Otherwise the corridor may acquire one emotional register: everyone arguing about allocation. This review strengthens the foundation; it does not yet prove a distinctive full setting or calibrated authorial voice.
